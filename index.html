Apollo 11 Lunar Module "Eagle" Descent Stage
Target Coordinates: 0.67408° N, 23.47297° E • Mare Tranquillitatis
Dormant Monument
TRANSMISSION ARCHIVE SIMULATOR
"Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed."
🔬 Scientific Legacy & Breakthrough

Delivered the first humans to the Moon and deployed the Laser Ranging Retroreflector (LRRR). Scientists still bounce observatory lasers off this reflector today to measure the Moon's orbital drift with millimeter accuracy.

🚀 Why Was It Left Behind?

The rocket equation dictates extreme weight economy: launching heavy landing legs and descent engines back to lunar orbit would require double the fuel. Leaving the descent stage behind allowed the crew to lift off safely.

⏳ Current State on the Surface

Preserved in the pristine lunar vacuum under extreme thermal swings (-130°C to +120°C). Covered in microscopic micrometeorite pits, with its commemorative plaque permanently reading: "Here men from the planet Earth first set foot upon the Moon."

💡 "Did You Know?" (Youth STEM Fact)

The lunar module descent engine was so powerful that it created a blast crater beneath the lander, and the gold Kapton foil blanket was assembled by hand to insulate the fuel tanks!
